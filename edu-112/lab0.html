💭 THINK FIRST · GETTING INTO THE INVESTIGATION

The incident that brought you here left no malware and no stolen credentials — just a prompt and a response. Before you touch a single dashboard, it's worth thinking about why that changes how you investigate.

  • The incident has no malware and no stolen password — so why does an AI security analyst still need dedicated portal access at all?
    Q1 — MODEL ANSWER
    Because the "attack" here isn't a network intrusion — it's a data-handling event that only shows up in application-layer telemetry: what was typed into a Gen AI app, and what came back. Portal access gets you to the dashboards that log prompts and responses and the app inventory behind them. A traditional firewall or EDR view wouldn't show any of this, since nothing left the browser looking like malware traffic.
  • Why does the lab route you through two separate consoles — AI Security (AI Protect) and Zguard (AI Guard) — instead of one unified dashboard?
    Q2 — MODEL ANSWER
    They answer different questions. AI Protect is about visibility — what AI is being used, by whom, and how risky the discovered assets are. AI Guard is about real-time enforcement — what happened to one specific prompt/response and why. Splitting them mirrors the actual investigative flow: scope the problem first (Protect), then examine the specific policy decision (Guard).
