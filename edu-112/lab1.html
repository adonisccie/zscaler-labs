Lab 1: Discover and Analyze AI Usage (AI Protect)

LAB 1

Scenario: Now that you have access to the SDC, your first job is to understand the scope of AI usage across your organization. Leadership assumes this is mostly a browser problem, but the AI Security Platform quickly shows a broader reality: public AI activity spans browser-based Gen AI apps and endpoint-based Desktop AI apps. Explore AI Protect to build a baseline of where AI is being used before deciding how risk should be managed.

  • Navigate the AI security dashboard and identify major reporting zones
  • Review the App Inventory page to evaluate risk posture for discovered AI applications
  • Inspect AI assets, model risk, and workload drilldowns
  • Use usage insights to establish an AI adoption baseline
Prerequisites: Complete Lab 0
Time: ~15 minutes
💭 THINK FIRST · MAPPING AI USAGE

Before you dive into dashboards, consider what "AI usage" actually means once you look past the browser — and what a risk number does and doesn't tell you.

  • Leadership assumes AI usage is "mostly a browser problem." What does the Desktop AI / Gen AI split in App Inventory prove wrong about that assumption?
    Q1 — MODEL ANSWER
    Desktop AI apps are discovered through endpoint telemetry, not browser traffic — meaning employees are running AI tools (installed clients, OS-level assistants) that never touch a proxy log. If leadership only watched web traffic, an entire category of AI usage — and its risk — would be invisible.
  • A model's Risk bucket (Critical/High/Medium/Low) measures how likely it is to be exploited by adversarial attacks. Why is that a different question from "how sensitive is the data this model touches"?
    Q2 — MODEL ANSWER
    One measures attack surface (can this model be jailbroken or prompt-injected); the other measures blast radius (what happens if it is). A low-exploit-risk model that touches no sensitive data is a minor concern; the same exploit risk sitting on a workload full of PII or SSNs is a much bigger one — you need both numbers, not just one.
  • Why trace a model like gpt-5-pro down to its Workloads and then to Sensitive Data, instead of stopping at "this model is Unsanctioned"?
    Q3 — MODEL ANSWER
    "Unsanctioned" tells you a policy gap exists, but not what's at stake. Following the graph to the workload and its DLP hits (SSNs, tax IDs, CCPA-flagged files) turns an abstract inventory finding into a concrete, prioritizable risk — the same document trail an auditor or incident responder would need.

In this task, you get your first view of AI Protect. You start at the dashboard to see how Public AI usage is summarized and how AI applications are grouped into Gen AI and Desktop AI, so you can understand the overall shape of AI activity before you dive into details.

  • Navigate to the AI Dashboard: select the AI Security icon on the Zidentity dashboard to access AI Protect.
The AI Dashboard: three summary panels up top, then Public AI / AI Infrastructure / AI Red Teaming usage tabs below.
  • Review the top three panels on the AI Dashboard: Public AI Traffic Overview, AI Application by Categories, AI Assets by Categories.

Note: Each panel includes a Details link that opens a more detailed view of the underlying data.

  • Explore the details of each panel to observe which AI tools are in use and how AI assets are organized:
    • Navigate to the Public AI Traffic Overview panel's Details to see how AI traffic is summarized
    • Select Dashboard from the left navigation to return
    • Select Details for AI Application Categories and AI Assets by Categories to gain further insight
  • Explore the Public AI Overview: go to Public AI > Overview, and set the Date Range filter to Last 15 days.
  • Review high-level AI usage panels: Total Volume of AI traffic, Total Users, Sensitive Data events, and Files Uploaded.
  • Examine AI application inventory tiles: select See All in the Desktop AI Apps panel to view the full list.

Note: Notice the number of versions of Microsoft Copilot M365 detected in the inventory, so you understand how many different builds of a single AI tool may actually be in use. When you scale that across the entire organization, the numbers are staggering.

  • Analyze App Usage Trends to see which Gen AI apps are most active — change the filter on the Public AI > Overview dashboard to see which apps generate the most traffic by Volume, Transactions, or Users.

Verify: You have opened the AI Security dashboard, identified the three major panels that summarize Public AI usage, drilled into their details, and explored the Public AI overview. You now have a high-level picture of AI traffic volume, user activity, sensitive data events, and key applications that will guide deeper investigation.

In this task, you move from the high-level overview to a granular view. Here, you shift to the endpoint perspective to see which AI applications are detected through endpoint telemetry, not just through browser traffic. This helps you understand how AI usage extends beyond chatbot usage.

  • Explore the Top Desktop AI Apps panel:
    • Go to Public AI > App Inventory from the left-side navigation panel
    • Select the Desktop AI tab
    • Review the list of AI applications identified through endpoint telemetry, then select an application to inspect its detected versions and inventory metadata
  • Explore the Top Gen AI Apps:
    • Select the Gen AI tab
    • Select a Gen AI application to inspect its General Information, Hosting Information, Security Information, and Risk Index metadata

Verify: You have navigated to Public AI > App Inventory, reviewed Desktop AI and Gen AI applications, and inspected inventory details. You now see how endpoint-based Desktop AI apps extend visibility beyond browser traffic and how application metadata supports risk assessment.

In this task, you move from applications to AI assets. You explore how AI models and workloads are organized and assessed for risk, so you can connect high-level usage back to the specific AI components that process data.

  • Explore AI Resources: Models:
    • Go to AI Infrastructure > AI Assets from the left-side navigation panel
    • Confirm that the Models tab is selected
    • In the Models By Risk panel, review how models are grouped into risk buckets: Critical, High, Medium, Low, Unknown

Note: Risk here reflects how likely a model is to be exploited by adversarial attacks (such as prompt injection, jailbreaks, and data extraction). It is distinct from the Risk Level used on other assets, where the score measures the severity of open issues rather than attack resistance.

  • In the Models By Subtype panel, review how models are classified by subtype — for example, Foundation: a pretrained base model shipped by a provider such as GPT-4.0, Claude, or Llama.
  • In the models table, review core identification details about the model, such as type, environment, and source.

Note: As you review subtypes, connect them back to the kinds of AI applications you saw earlier — this helps you see which foundational models may sit behind high-traffic apps.

  • Explore the Model Details View:
    • In the Search box, type deep and press Enter
    • Select DeepSeek-V3 to open its detail view and review the General Information
    • Explore the Graph View toward the bottom of the Model Details View
    • Select the icon for Access, AI model, or Workloads to expand a card with more detail about the asset
    • Trace how the model connects to access paths and workloads — this prepares you to think about how a single model can affect multiple parts of your AI landscape
Graph View for a model (gpt-5-pro shown as an example): select any node — Access, the model itself, or Workloads — to expand its detail panel on the right.
  • Explore AI Resources: Workloads:
    • Go to AI Infrastructure > AI Resources from the left-side navigation panel
    • Select the Workloads tab
AI Resources — Workloads tab: 85 workloads total, 22 with public exposure, 20 encrypted with PMK.
  • Narrow the Workloads Table results with the Risk Level filter by selecting Critical.
  • Select the acme-corp-dev-mcp-server-01 workload.
  • In the General information panel, note the sensitive data exposed for this workload. Consider how this exposure relates to the AI usage patterns you saw earlier.
  • Observe the Graph View for the workload, then select the Sensitive Data node — a details pane appears on the right.
Selecting the Sensitive Data node on a workload's Graph View surfaces the DLP engines that matched — here, DPDPA, CCPA-High Volume, and predefined SSN/Tax ID dictionaries.
  • Select View Data Security on the bottom right of the details pane, then observe the matched files, number of triggers, DLP engines, DLP dictionaries, document categories, document types, and the file table.
The full Data Security view for the workload: 15 matched files and 5,804 triggers across DLP engines, dictionaries, document categories, and types — down to individual file paths.

Note: You can use this information to connect workloads to the kinds of sensitive data they handle, as well as the policies that are violated. In a production environment this is important when you evaluate controls and enforcement.

Verify: You have explored AI assets by reviewing models grouped by risk and subtype, opened a model's details and Graph View, and examined a high-risk workload's exposure of sensitive data, including data security details. You now understand how AI Protect links abstract models and workloads to concrete data risk.

🧠
MENTAL NOTE
AI Protect = visibility. Three reporting zones: Public AI (Gen AI + Desktop AI usage), AI Infrastructure (AI Assets — Models / Workloads / Data Stores), and AI Red Teaming (coverage). Model Risk buckets (Critical/High/Medium/Low/Unknown) measure exploitability, not sensitivity — always follow the graph from model → workload → Sensitive Data to see what's actually at stake.

LAB PROGRESS
QUICK REFERENCE ▲
Gen AI app
A browser-based public generative AI tool (ChatGPT, Claude, etc.) seen in Public AI traffic.
Desktop AI app
An AI application detected via endpoint telemetry rather than browser traffic.
AI Asset
A discovered model, agent, or app that AI Security tracks and risk-scores.
Model Risk buckets
Critical / High / Medium / Low / Unknown — how likely a model is to be exploited by adversarial attacks, not how sensitive its data is.
Foundation model
A pretrained base model subtype shipped by a provider, e.g. GPT-4.0, Claude, or Llama.
Workload
An AI-related compute resource (e.g. an MCP server) tracked for public exposure, encryption, and sensitive-data handling.
DLP engine / dictionary
Data-loss-prevention rule sets (DPDPA, CCPA, SSN, etc.) that match sensitive content inside files or workloads.
