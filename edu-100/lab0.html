Lab 0: Connect to the Virtual Lab Environment

OVERVIEW

Scenario: Your training pod hosts a dedicated copy of the Labtenant environment, and every configuration task in this course happens there. Before you begin, you must be able to reach the pod, move text between the lab guide and the lab virtual machines, and sign in to the Experience Center console that manages the Zscaler services.

By the end of this lab, you will be able to:

  • Sign in to the CloudShare lab environment and confirm that the lab VMs are running.
  • Send text from the lab guide to a virtual machine with the lab clipboard.
  • Sign in to the Corp Client PC and the Experience Center console, and locate features with the Search menu.

Do this first: Enter your Student FQDN and Student Password in the Lab Configuration panel above and select Save. Add your first and last name too; Lab 1 uses them. Every EDU-100 lab page reads these values, so you only enter them once.

Success criteria

  • ☐ You're logged in to CloudShare and the lab VMs show as running.
  • ☐ You found the Lab Guide tab and the Zscaler tenant Credentials.
  • ☐ Text sent with Send Text appears in Notepad++ on the Corp Client PC.
  • ☐ You're signed in to console.zscaler.com as the student admin and can use the Search menu.

Watch out for

  • ⚠ The VMs can take a few minutes to start. Wait for them before you continue.
  • ⚠ Your Student FQDN is everything after the @ in your student admin username.
  • ⚠ Use Send Text instead of typing long values by hand; small typos cause confusing failures later.

💭 THINK FIRST · YOUR LAB ENVIRONMENT

This lab is mostly orientation, but the ideas behind it come back in every lab: your own tenant, one console, and values you'll reuse again and again.

  • Each student gets a dedicated pod and a unique Student FQDN. Why not have everyone share one tenant?
    Q1 — MODEL ANSWER
    Isolation. In a shared tenant, one student's SSL rule, URL block or access policy would affect everyone else, and nobody could tell whose change broke what. With your own pod, the policies you write apply only to your users. The Student FQDN is the domain those users belong to: the student admin, and the user you create in Lab 1, are both name@<Student FQDN>.
  • The Experience Center is one console for ZIA, ZPA and ZDX, and it routes you to your tenant based on your login. What does a single entry point give an administrator?
    Q2 — MODEL ANSWER
    One sign-in (with one set of authentication controls, such as MFA) instead of a separate portal per service, and one place to work across services. Your login ID identifies which tenant you manage, so the console sends you to the right one. The Search menu reaches features in every service, which is why each later lab starts with "use the Search menu to find…".
  • Why does the course have you send text into the VMs with the lab clipboard instead of typing values yourself?
    Q3 — MODEL ANSWER
    The VMs run in CloudShare, not on your computer, so your normal clipboard doesn't reach them directly. Many lab values are long and exact (login IDs, URLs, CLI commands). A single wrong character in a login ID or command doesn't fail where you typed it; it shows up later as a sign-in or policy that mysteriously doesn't work. Sending the text avoids that.

Welcome to the Introduction to Zscaler Zero Trust Exchange (EDU-100) lab. In this lab, you apply the skills you learned during the instructor-led training by working in a guided lab environment. By the end of the lab, you will be able to:

  • Connect to the virtual lab environment and sign in to the Zscaler Experience Center console.
  • Create a user group and a user, and assign a service entitlement in the Service Portal (formerly ZIdentity).
  • Forward traffic from a Windows client to the Zero Trust Exchange with Zscaler Client Connector.
  • Enable SSL/TLS inspection and enforce URL Filtering policy with Zscaler Internet Access (ZIA).
  • Provide Zero Trust access to a private application with Zscaler Private Access (ZPA).
  • Monitor a SaaS application with Zscaler Digital Experience (ZDX).

Lab Description and Scenario

For these lab exercises, you work with a scenario in which your organization, Labtenant, is rolling out the Zscaler Zero Trust Exchange to protect users from Internet threats, control Internet access, and provide secure access to private applications without a VPN. Labtenant also wants visibility into the digital experience of its users. You deploy Zscaler Client Connector (ZCC) on the Corp Client PC so that a single agent forwards Internet traffic to ZIA, private application traffic to ZPA, and experience monitoring data to ZDX.

To support the rollout, you are tasked to:

  • Onboard users and assign service entitlements in the Service Portal (formerly ZIdentity).
  • Connect the Windows client to the Zero Trust Exchange by configuring forwarding and app profiles for ZCC.
  • Enable SSL/TLS inspection and block risky URL categories for Internet traffic.
  • Provision a ZPA App Connector and publish an intranet application to authorized users only.
  • Configure ZDX Web and Cloud Path probes to monitor a business-critical SaaS application.

Lab Setup

You will have access to Zscaler Zero Trust Exchange and a CloudShare pod. For your lab exercises, you will configure the Service Portal (formerly ZIdentity), ZIA, ZPA, and ZDX services and explore the end user experience. You will use a Windows client PC (Corp Client PC) hosted in CloudShare to connect to the Zero Trust Exchange.

Lab Topology

The Corp Client PC reaches the Internet and the Corporate Data Center (10.0.0.0/24) through the Zero Trust Exchange. You manage ZIA, ZPA and ZDX from the Experience Center.

Lab Access Information

For this course, each student is assigned a unique student ID with a dedicated virtual environment (hosted in CloudShare) and corresponding Student FQDN along with a set of admin and test user accounts for Zscaler. User credentials and portal addresses are all contained in the lab access instructions provided to you for your assigned training pod. These include:

  • Your Student ID, which is a unique identifier for your pod (e.g., zs9901)
  • Your Student FQDN, which is a unique domain for your pod (e.g., zs9901.labtenant.com)
  • Your admin login credentials for access to the Zscaler portals:
    • The login name will be in the format student@<Student FQDN>, where <Student FQDN> is the domain assigned to your pod. For example, student@zsxxxx.labtenant.com.
    • The password is unique for your session and is set as the same password on all the configured admin and user accounts for the pod.

    Use Go to my VM List, or a VM's tab across the top.

    Credentials > Zscaler Tenant: the console URL, your student admin username and its password (hidden in this example). Copy your Student FQDN and password into the Lab Configuration panel.

The Zscaler Experience Center (console.zscaler.com) serves as the unified entry point for all Zscaler services, routing administrators to the correct tenant based on their login credentials. It supports multiple authentication methods including MFA via security keys and biometrics, and provides a centralized dashboard to manage ZIA, ZPA, and ZDX configurations. See What Is Zscaler Experience Center? ↗

The Zscaler Experience Center provides a unified interface for administrators to onboard users, navigate product features, and manage tenants. The console's Search bar enables quick access to any feature across ZIA, ZPA, and ZDX, while the Analytics and Administration panels provide centralized visibility and configuration capabilities. See Onboarding Users to Experience Center ↗
