OPTIONAL LAB

ZTB logs sit next to ZIA and ZPA telemetry in the Experience Center, and the 8.1.2 Health Monitor adds system and WAN health without logging in to the gateway.

Scenario: Operations wants proof that policies are enforced and an early warning when the branch degrades.

In this lab you will:

  • Filter Packet and Flow logs for your pod.
  • Review alarms and Web Insights.
  • Read service, system and WAN health, including the WAN score.

Success criteria — tick when done

  • ☐ Packet and Flow logs filtered by pod##.
  • ☐ Alarms and a Web Insights query viewed.
  • ☐ WAN score, loss, latency and jitter recorded for ge1 and ge2.

Watch out for

  • ⚠ Logs empty — widen the time range; check the Policy filter value is pod##.
  • ⚠ Health shows white — metrics arrive every 60 seconds; wait or widen the range.

💭 THINK FIRST · MONITOR THE BRANCH

Monitoring answers two questions: did the policy fire, and is the branch healthy?

  • Why filter logs by your pod number?
    Q1 — MODEL ANSWER
    The tenant is shared by every student in the class, so filtering shows only the logs created by your own policies.
  • How do Packet logs differ from Flow logs?
    Q2 — MODEL ANSWER
    Packet logs record individual packets that matched ZTB policies for forensic detail; Flow logs summarize whole connections with bytes and the matching policy.
  • What makes up the WAN score?
    Q3 — MODEL ANSWER
    The WANmon score is built from loss, latency and jitter of the path.
